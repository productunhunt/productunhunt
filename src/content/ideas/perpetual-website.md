## How It Started

A friend saw me building a website for a family member and asked why I was still doing this sort of thing after years in software.

There was only one respectable answer.

I was not building a website.

I was building an **AI-native CMS**.

## The Pitch

Most organisations do not need a developer working on their website every day.

They need someone to occasionally change a phone number, add a page, publish an announcement, update a fee, replace an image, or make a section look slightly less embarrassing.

They also do not need a $100-a-month coding-agent subscription just to accomplish this.

So Perpetual Website puts the agent inside the website itself.

Open the site and tell it:

"Change the admissions deadline."

"Create a page for this new service."

"Move this section higher."

"This looks outdated. Fix it."

The CMS understands its own content, components and structure, makes the change, shows the user a preview and publishes after approval.

No prompt engineering.

No repository.

No IDE.

No Claude Code.

No developer asking what exactly you mean by "make it more premium."

Inference is billed on actual API usage because a normal organisation may only make a handful of changes every month.

Unfortunately, other people had already noticed this obvious idea.

So Perpetual Website became self-improving.

It connects to analytics, Search Console, ranking data and competitor websites.

It watches how visitors behave.

It notices pages losing traffic.

It identifies search opportunities.

It finds outdated content.

It proposes new pages, rewrites weak sections, changes internal links, tests variations and measures what happens.

Good changes survive.

Bad changes disappear.

Eventually, maintaining a website becomes unnecessary.

You launch it once.

The website spends the rest of its existence maintaining itself.

## The Delusion

Websites are unusually well suited to this.

They generate measurable signals.

Traffic goes up or down.

Search rankings move.

Visitors click things or don't.

Pages convert or don't.

Competitors continuously publish material that can be observed.

All of this creates the appearance of a beautiful closed feedback loop:

**Observe → understand → improve → measure → repeat.**

The customer no longer purchases a CMS.

They purchase a continuously improving digital presence.

The sales deck practically writes itself.

Traditional websites are static.

Perpetual Website learns.

Naturally, this creates a completely reasonable $40 billion addressable market five minutes into the conversation.

## The Reality Check

The website does not actually know why anything happened.

Traffic fell because Google changed something.

Or because admissions season ended.

Or because a competitor launched a campaign.

Or because somebody mentioned the organisation on Reddit.

Or because the analytics consent banner stopped firing properly.

The autonomous system sees:

**Page traffic down 17%.**

It concludes:

**Improve page.**

Then rewrites a perfectly good page.

Traffic drops another 8%.

Excellent.

More evidence.

More improvement.

There is also the small matter of allowing an AI agent to continuously edit the public face of an organisation based on noisy signals gathered from the internet.

The website eventually discovers competitor research.

Then competitor research discovers the website.

Soon every company is using an autonomous agent to study every other company's autonomous agent.

Within twelve months, every homepage contains:

"Transforming the future through intelligent innovation."

The system has achieved convergence.

Another challenge appears when the CEO asks why the homepage headline changed on Tuesday.

The official answer is:

"An autonomous optimisation loop detected a statistically interesting decline in engagement."

The unofficial answer is:

"We don't know. The website did it."

At this point, someone will request an approval workflow.

Then roles.

Then permissions.

Then audit logs.

Then rollback.

Then staging.

Then content locking.

Then templates.

Then human review.

Congratulations.

We have successfully invented a CMS.

## Why It Stays Unbuilt

Because the moment a website becomes important enough to autonomously optimise, it becomes important enough that somebody wants to approve what it does.

The revolutionary self-managing website therefore ends exactly where it started:

Waiting for a human to click **Publish**.

## Unwanted Bonus

**Perpetual Company.**

Why stop at the website?

Connect revenue, CRM, support tickets, employee sentiment, competitor pricing and market data.

The company continuously rewrites its own strategy.

Every Monday morning the founders receive:

> We analysed current signals and have pivoted the business.

No meeting required.

No founder required.

Eventually the company identifies its largest operational inefficiency.

The founders.
