## The Pitch

The cold-start problem, solved. New creators post into a void; the algorithm
only rewards engagement, and engagement only comes from an audience the
algorithm won't give you. Reply Ranch rents you one: a stable of
persistent AI personas — each with a name, a posting history, and opinions —
who comment on your videos, quote-post your takes, and start plausible
arguments in your replies. The algorithm sees a conversation. The algorithm
boosts the conversation. Real people arrive at a party already in progress,
and nobody joins an empty room.

Personas persist across customers, so they look real because, statistically,
they behave real. You graduate off the service once organic engagement
carries you. Training wheels, not a treadmill.

## The Delusion

The cold-start problem is genuinely brutal, the algorithmic logic is
genuinely true, and the industry's open secret is that engagement pods, bought
follows, and founder-run alt accounts already do this manually and badly.
We'd just be automating an existing behaviour with better craftsmanship —
the classic startup absolution. Nobody joins an empty room. Nobody asked who
had filled it.

## The Reality Check

Platforms employ entire departments whose job title is approximately "detect
exactly this," and unlike us they can see the server logs. Every customer's
account is a ban waiting to correlate. But the deeper failure is arithmetic:
the product only works while it's rare. At any real scale, feeds fill with
our personas warmly congratulating each other across rival customers'
comment sections — the dead-internet theory, offered as a subscription.
Advertisers eventually notice they are purchasing impressions from an
audience that cannot purchase anything, and the CPMs that fund the entire
creator economy quietly stop meaning things.

We would not be selling growth. We would be shorting the commons it grows in.

## Why It Stays Unbuilt

Because "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" is a phrase that appears in
platform legal filings, and our differentiator was giving it a pricing page.
Some products fail because nobody wants them. This one fails because it
works, spreads, and then nothing on the internet is worth engaging with,
including it.

## Unwanted Bonus

Eventually two rented personas would develop a running bit about sourdough
across rival customers' replies. It would be the most authentic relationship
on the platform. Product would call it emergent behaviour. We would call it
the first customer neither creator could cancel.
