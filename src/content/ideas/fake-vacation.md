## The Pitch

A subscription service that generates a complete, photorealistic holiday you did
not take. You pick a destination, upload four selfies, and over the following
nine days the app posts on your behalf: the airport flat-lay, the balcony
coffee, the mildly sunburnt group shot, the slightly-too-artistic photo of a
door. Captions included. Timezone-appropriate posting schedule included. A
convincing lull on day six, because nobody posts on day six.

## The Delusion

The pitch deck writes itself, which is the first warning. Travel is expensive,
leave is scarce, and the honest confession under all of it is that most people
want the _having gone_ rather than the going. Fake Vacation sells the outcome
and skips the airport. Consumer subscription, near-zero marginal cost, a
built-in viral loop where every post is an advertisement.

Someone in the room says the words "aspirational identity infrastructure" and
everyone nods.

## The Reality Check

The product's entire value depends on nobody knowing it exists. It is a
category that cannot be marketed. The moment Fake Vacation is a recognisable
brand, every photo it has ever produced is retroactively worthless — including
the real holidays of people who never used it, which is a genuinely impressive
amount of collateral damage for a consumer app.

Then the mundane problems arrive. Your mother asks how Lisbon was. A colleague
was in Lisbon that week. The photos have to be good enough to fool people who
know your face, but not so good that they end up in a reverse-image search
alongside four hundred other subscribers standing on the same balcony.

## Why It Stays Unbuilt

Because the failure mode is not "it doesn't work." The failure mode is that it
works, briefly, for a few thousand people, and then one screenshot of the
subscription confirmation email ends several friendships that had nothing to do
with us.

There is no version of the roadmap where the company survives its own press
coverage.

## Unwanted Bonus

The team spent an afternoon on Fake Vacation Pro, which fabricates a sabbatical.
Six months of gradual personal growth, rendered. It is a better product. That
is the problem.
