## The Pitch

Hiring a barista requires two references. Dating requires a phone and an
optimistic bio. Exes & References closes the gap: to activate a profile, two
former partners answer a short structured questionnaire — communication,
reliability, how they behave when a restaurant loses the booking. Answers are
summarised into a calm, neutral reference card. No free text, no revenge
essays. Verified humans, verified history.

The tagline tested extremely well: "Date someone with a paper trail."

## The Delusion

Trust is a familiar problem in online dating, and every incumbent
solves it with a blue tick that confirms only that your face exists.
Exes & References verifies the thing people actually want verified: what you are
like after month three. The exes are a free, infinitely renewable content
source. The screenshots market themselves. Somebody said "LinkedIn for love"
and, worryingly, nobody laughed.

## The Reality Check

Walk through the onboarding as a real person. Step one: contact two exes.
Step two: ask them for a favour. There is no step three, because the funnel
has selected for exactly two groups: people on miraculously good terms with
everyone they've ever dated, and people whose references are their cousin with
a new area code. We never measured the drop-off; dignity prevented the test.
The structured questionnaire reduces revenge essays and also prevents
information; every card converges on "communicative, mostly punctual," which
is a horoscope.

Meanwhile the failure modes write themselves: reference-swapping rings,
defamation letters, and the breakup conversation acquiring a new closing
item — "so, will you still fill out my card?"

## Why It Stays Unbuilt

Because the product's core loop depends on a favour from the single person on
Earth least motivated to grant it. Marketplaces die when one side won't show
up, and we designed a marketplace whose supply side is, by definition, over
us.

## Unwanted Bonus

The v2 backlog contained "endorsements" — one-tap skills for exes to confirm,
LinkedIn-style. Top requested example in the concept test: "good at
assembling furniture." The furniture people may have been onto something.
That app we would use.
